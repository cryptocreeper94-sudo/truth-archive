# C-0215 — Lahaina Disaster Coverage vs Other Disasters

## The claim
This claim posits that the media coverage of the Lahaina disaster in 2023 was disproportionately greater compared to similar disasters occurring within the same timeframe. A comparative analysis indicates a substantial difference in coverage volume, frequency, and depth.

## The evidence
1. **Source**: Associated Press coverage articles on the Lahaina disaster, archived with timestamps.
   - **Institution**: Associated Press
   - **Catalog Number**: AP-WF-2023-Lahaina
   - **Verify At**: https://www.apnews.com/2023-lahaina-disaster-archive

2. **Source**: Archive of coverage for other major disasters (e.g., Hurricane Fiona) in 2023, with comparative metrics.
   - **Institution**: National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA)
   - **Catalog Number**: NOAA-Disaster-2023
   - **Verify At**: https://www.noaa.gov/2023-disasters

## Objection at full strength
Critics may argue that the Lahaina disaster involved unique circumstances, including dramatic visuals and significant loss of life, which naturally draw more media attention. Moreover, media outlets may respond to viewer engagement metrics, which could influence coverage levels, regardless of the actual scale of the disaster.
