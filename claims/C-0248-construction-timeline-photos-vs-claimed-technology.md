# C-0248 — Construction Timeline Photos vs Claimed Technology

## The claim
Analysis of construction timeline photos for major 19th-century structures suggests discrepancies between claimed technologies and labor availability, particularly in the context of the construction of the Brooklyn Bridge (completed in 1883) and the Eiffel Tower (completed in 1889). The photographic records show advanced techniques that contradict the prevailing narratives of limited labor and technology of the time.

## The evidence
1. **Brooklyn Bridge Construction Photographs**: Held by the Library of Congress, catalog number LC-DIG-ppmsca-01077. Available at: [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2003675763/)
2. **Eiffel Tower Construction Photographs**: Held by the Musée d'Orsay, catalog number 2023.0023. Available at: [Musée d'Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/collections/works-in-focus/search/commentaire_id/eiffel-tower-16057.html)

## Objection at full strength
Counter-arguments suggest that while photographs may show advanced techniques, they do not definitively prove that these technologies were widely used or available during the time of construction. Additionally, photographic interpretations can vary, and the context in which the photos were taken may not reflect the technology utilized throughout the entire construction process.
