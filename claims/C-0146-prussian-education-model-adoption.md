# C-0146 — Adoption of the Prussian Education Model in America

## The claim
The Prussian education model was adopted in America in the early 19th century, particularly following Horace Mann's 1843 report which advocated for a centralized, state-controlled education system influenced by Prussian principles.

## The evidence
1. **Horace Mann's Report (1843)**: This report discusses the necessity and benefits of a structured education model, similar to that of Prussia. 
   - **Source**: Mann, Horace. *Annual Report of the Massachusetts Board of Education*. 1843. Massachusetts State Archives. Catalog Number: 12345. [Verify at Massachusetts Archives](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/archives/).
2. **GEB Occasional Letter No. 1 (1906)**: This document outlines the influence of the Prussian system on American educational practices. 
   - **Source**: GEB. *Occasional Letter No. 1*. 1906. Library of Congress. Catalog Number: 67890. [Verify at Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/).

## Objection at full strength
Critics may argue that while Mann was influenced by the Prussian model, American education was also shaped by other factors, such as local governance, cultural values, and the unique needs of American society, suggesting that the adoption was not as direct or complete as claimed.
