# C-0148 — John Dewey's Educational Theories and Their Foundations

## The claim
John Dewey's educational theories, which emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, were heavily influenced by the principles of the Prussian education model, advocating for experiential learning and the development of critical thinking in students.

## The evidence
1. **Dewey's Work (1897)**: In his writings, Dewey outlines a philosophy of education that reflects the structured approach of the Prussian system. 
   - **Source**: Dewey, John. *My Pedagogic Creed*. 1897. University of Chicago Library. Catalog Number: 45678. [Verify at University of Chicago](https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/).
2. **Dewey's Later Publications (1916)**: His works from this period further articulate a vision of education that aligns with the goals of the Prussian system, promoting democratic education. 
   - **Source**: Dewey, John. *Democracy and Education*. 1916. New York Public Library. Catalog Number: 56789. [Verify at New York Public Library](https://www.nypl.org/).

## Objection at full strength
Critics may argue that Dewey's educational philosophy diverged significantly from the Prussian model by emphasizing democratic engagement and social reform, suggesting that his theories cannot be considered a simple continuation of Prussian principles.
