## The claim
The construction methods employed at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair, known as the White City, utilized innovative techniques and materials that allowed for rapid building of the exhibition structures. Observers noted the extensive use of steel framing and prefabricated components, claiming this approach was revolutionary for the time.

## The evidence
- S-0659: 1893 Chicago World's Fair Construction Report, held at the Chicago Historical Society, catalog number CHS-1893-CW.
- S-0660: Photographic Documentation of the White City, held at the Library of Congress, catalog number LOC-1893-WC.

## Objection at full strength
Critics argue that the reported construction methods may have been exaggerated for promotional purposes, citing that many structures were later deemed temporary and lacked the durability implied. Furthermore, some experts suggest that traditional masonry techniques were still heavily employed in many buildings, contradicting claims of widespread innovation.
