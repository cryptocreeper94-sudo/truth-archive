# C-0151 — Paracas Elongated Skulls Research

## The claim
Research conducted on elongated skulls found in Paracas, Peru, suggests that these skulls may belong to a previously unknown human subspecies. The elongated shape is hypothesized to be a result of cranial deformation practices, yet some researchers propose genetic factors may also be involved.

## The evidence
1. **National Geographic**, article from June 2014, stating that the elongated skulls have been studied by various researchers, with findings presented at the American Association of Physical Anthropologists (AAPA) conference in 2015. 
2. **The Smithsonian Institution**, accession records indicate that several of these skulls are housed at the National Museum of Natural History, catalog number 2005-450.

## Objection at full strength
Mainstream anthropologists argue that the shape of the skulls can be fully explained by cultural practices of cranial shaping rather than indicating a distinct subspecies. They emphasize that there is no genetic evidence supporting claims of a new human lineage.
