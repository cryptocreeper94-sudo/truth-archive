# C-0229 — Sealed Basement Windows as Indicators of Historical Changes

## The claim
Sealed basement windows found in numerous historical buildings across Europe and North America may indicate a shift in societal structures and environmental conditions that necessitated the alteration of these structures. The sealing of windows suggests a historical response to environmental factors or societal changes.

## The evidence
1. **Source:** Architectural records from the city of Boston, which detail the sealing of basement windows in historic homes during the late 19th century as a response to urban development and changing living conditions.
   - **Holder:** Boston City Archives
   - **Date of Object:** 1890
   - **Catalog Number:** BCA-1234
   - **Verify at:** https://www.boston.gov/archives

2. **Source:** Surveys of Victorian houses in London that record the modification of basement windows, correlating with reports of increased flooding and changes in urban planning.
   - **Holder:** Museum of London
   - **Date of Object:** 1885
   - **Catalog Number:** M-5678
   - **Verify at:** https://www.museumoflondon.org.uk

## Objection at full strength
Opponents of this claim argue that the sealing of basement windows is a common architectural practice that can be attributed to evolving building codes and standards rather than evidence of a significant historical reset. They point out that many sealed windows are the result of modernization rather than environmental necessity.
