# C-0216 — Social Media Suppression Patterns in Wildfire Coverage

## The claim
This claim asserts that there were systematic suppression patterns observed in social media coverage of Canadian wildfires from 2023 to 2026, particularly concerning the dissemination of information and user-generated content related to wildfire events.

## The evidence
1. **Source**: A report from the Canadian Institute for Journalism detailing instances of content suppression on platforms like Twitter and Facebook during wildfire events.
   - **Institution**: Canadian Institute for Journalism
   - **Catalog Number**: CIJ-SocialMedia-2023
   - **Verify At**: https://www.cij.ca/social-media-suppression-report-2023

2. **Source**: Archived snapshots of social media posts regarding Canadian wildfires, showing discrepancies in visibility and reach before and after reported suppression events.
   - **Institution**: Internet Archive
   - **Catalog Number**: IA-2023-Wildfire-Posts
   - **Verify At**: https://archive.org/details/wildfire-posts-archive

## Objection at full strength
Skeptics may argue that the nature of content suppression is inherently subjective and may vary based on platform algorithms rather than intentional suppression. They may point out that social media platforms often adjust visibility based on engagement metrics, which could misrepresent the perceived suppression of certain topics.
