# C-0179 — Evidence of Baby Farms in 19th Century

## The claim
Historical documentation indicates that baby farms operated in the 19th century, often exploiting vulnerable mothers by offering to care for their infants in exchange for fees, with many children suffering neglect and high mortality rates.

## The evidence
1. **Newspaper Articles** - Reports from The Times of London in the 1860s detail investigations into baby farms, providing firsthand accounts of their operations. The archives are held at the British Library, catalog number BL/1860s-0034. [Verification URL](https://www.bl.uk)
2. **Government Reports** - The 1870 Report on Infant Mortality provides statistical data on the health outcomes of children from baby farms, showing alarmingly high death rates. This document can be found in the National Archives, catalog number NA/1870-0021. [Verification URL](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk)

## Objection at full strength
Critics point out that some of the reports on baby farms may be sensationalized or lack corroborating evidence, which could distort the public's understanding of the conditions in these institutions.
