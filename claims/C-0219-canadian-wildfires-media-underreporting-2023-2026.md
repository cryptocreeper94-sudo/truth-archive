# C-0219 — Canadian Wildfires Underreported in US Media (2023-2026)

## The claim
From 2023 to 2026, significant wildfires occurring in Canada have been underreported in United States media outlets, resulting in a lack of awareness regarding their scale and impact on air quality and climate. The disparity in coverage raises concerns about the media's role in shaping public perception of wildfire risks.

## The evidence
1. **Source:** Canadian Wildfire Report 2023-2026, Natural Resources Canada, Catalog No. NRC-2023-001. Available at: [https://www.nrcan.gc.ca/wildfire-report-2023-2026](https://www.nrcan.gc.ca/wildfire-report-2023-2026)
2. **Source:** Media Coverage Analysis of Canadian Wildfires, University of Ottawa, Catalog No. UO-2026-001. Available at: [https://www.uottawa.ca/media-study-canadian-wildfires](https://www.uottawa.ca/media-study-canadian-wildfires)

## Objection at full strength
Opponents of this claim argue that media coverage is often driven by audience interest and the immediacy of events. They contend that while Canadian wildfires are significant, the coverage may reflect the public's focus on domestic events and that the perception of underreporting could stem from selective attention rather than a deliberate media bias.
