# C-0152 — Ancient Tools in Unexpected Strata

## The claim
Numerous archaeological sites have reported the discovery of stone tools in geological strata that predate the known arrival of humans in those regions, suggesting a more complex timeline for human migration and tool use in North America.

## The evidence
1. **The Denver Post**, dated March 15, 1927, page 3: Report detailing the discovery of stone tools in a layer dated to 20,000 years ago near the Arkansas River. 
2. **Archaeology Magazine**, article from January/February 2015, discussing similar finds in the Clovis layer that challenge established timelines.

## Objection at full strength
Critics assert that these finds may be the result of natural processes that could have displaced the artifacts into older strata, or that the dating methods used are flawed. They emphasize that until more rigorous corroborative evidence is presented, claims of pre-Clovis artifacts should be treated with skepticism.
