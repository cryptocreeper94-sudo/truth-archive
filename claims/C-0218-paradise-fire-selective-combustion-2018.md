# C-0218 — Selective Combustion in Paradise, California (2018)

## The claim
During the Camp Fire in Paradise, California, in November 2018, a pattern of selective combustion was observed where vehicles with darker colors and certain roof materials were more likely to be destroyed than lighter-colored vehicles and structures. This raises questions about the influence of color and material composition on fire susceptibility.

## The evidence
1. **Source:** Camp Fire Incident Report, California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection, Catalog No. 2018-045. Available at: [https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2018/11/8/camp-fire/](https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2018/11/8/camp-fire/)
2. **Source:** Fire Behavior Analysis Report, U.S. Forest Service, Catalog No. USFS-2018-005. Available at: [https://www.fs.usda.gov/camp-fire-report-2018](https://www.fs.usda.gov/camp-fire-report-2018)

## Objection at full strength
Some experts claim that the selective destruction observed is purely anecdotal and lacks a scientific basis. They argue that numerous variables, including construction materials, vegetation, and fire intensity, were at play, making it difficult to attribute survival rates to color or material alone without thorough and controlled studies.
