# C-0188 — WTC 1/2/7 Freefall Analysis

## The claim
The freefall of WTC 7 for a period of approximately 2.25 seconds, as documented in the FEMA BPAT Report Appendix C, indicates that the building's collapse could not have been solely caused by office fires. The report highlights the sulfidation and intergranular melting of steel, suggesting that other factors were involved in the collapse.

## The evidence
1. **FEMA BPAT Report Appendix C** - This document provides detailed analysis of the WTC 7 collapse, including the unexpected freefall duration. 
   - **Institution**: National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
   - **Catalog Number**: NIST NCSTAR 1A
   - **Verification URL**: https://www.nist.gov/publications-archive

2. **University of Alaska Fairbanks WTC7 Finite Element Study (Hulsey 2020)** - This peer-reviewed study further analyzes the structural dynamics of WTC 7, supporting the claim that fires alone could not account for the collapse.
   - **Institution**: University of Alaska Fairbanks
   - **DOI**: 10.13140/RG.2.2.33507.13608
   - **Verification URL**: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335098839

## Objection at full strength
The NIST report states that the collapse was primarily due to thermal expansion of steel beams and connections, which led to the failure of key structural elements. Critics argue that the freefall duration could be attributed to the building’s design features that allowed for rapid descent under certain conditions, thus disputing the need for alternative explanations beyond fire-induced damage.
