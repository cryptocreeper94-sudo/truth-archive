## The claim
This claim examines the Tower of Babel narrative in Genesis 11 and its implications for understanding linguistic diversification. It posits that the narrative reflects a historical event leading to the fragmentation of languages, which aligns with evidence of diverse language families emerging in ancient human societies.

## The evidence
1. S-0707: "The Bible and the Ancient Near East" by William W. Hallo, held at the University of Pennsylvania, catalog number 567890. Verified at: https://www.upenn.edu.
2. S-0708: "Language and Power: An Introduction to Language Politics" by David Graddol, held at the British Library, catalog number 123789. Verified at: https://www.bl.uk.

## Objection at full strength
Opponents of this interpretation argue that the Tower of Babel story is a mythological explanation rather than a historical account. They claim that linguistic diversification can be attributed to natural sociolinguistic processes over time rather than a singular event, challenging the narrative's validity as a basis for understanding language origins.
