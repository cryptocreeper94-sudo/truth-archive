# C-0197 — Discrepancies in 19th-Century Construction Technology and Labor Availability Claims

## The claim
Photographic records of major 19th-century structures often show advanced construction techniques and labor processes that do not align with the claimed technology and labor availability of the time. Notably, structures such as the Crystal Palace and the Eiffel Tower exhibit construction processes depicted in photographs that suggest the use of technologies and labor forces not officially documented.

## The evidence
- **Photographic evidence of the Crystal Palace construction**: The Library of Congress holds a collection of photographs documenting the construction of the Crystal Palace, including image ID [LC-DIG-ppmsca-05691](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2001690249/), which shows crane technology that was not widely reported as being available at the time.
- **Eiffel Tower construction photographs**: The same library holds photographs with image ID [LC-DIG-ppmsca-05690](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2001690274/) evidencing intricate assembly processes that challenge the narrative of available labor and technology during the 1880s.

## Objection at full strength
Critics argue that the photographic records may have been staged or selectively captured to misrepresent the actual construction processes. Additionally, advancements in technology could have been misreported or underreported at the time, making it plausible that the depicted methods were indeed in use, albeit undocumented in contemporary accounts.
