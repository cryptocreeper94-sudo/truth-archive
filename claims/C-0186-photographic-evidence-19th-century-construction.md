# C-0186 — Photographic Evidence of 19th Century Construction Discrepancies

## The claim
Photographic records from the construction of major 19th-century structures show atmospheric anomalies, suggesting discrepancies between claimed construction timelines and available technology/labor. Specific examples include the construction of the Brooklyn Bridge (completed in 1883) and the St. Louis World's Fair (1904).

## The evidence
1. **Brooklyn Bridge Construction Photographs**
   - **Source**: Library of Congress, Catalog Number: LOT 13466, Image ID: LC-DIG-ppmsca-12105
   - **Verification URL**: https://www.loc.gov/item/2012645755/

2. **St. Louis World's Fair Construction Photographs**
   - **Source**: Missouri Historical Society, Catalog Number: 10547, Image ID: 10547
t   - **Verification URL**: https://mohistory.org/collections/item/10547

## Objection at full strength
Counter-arguments suggest that atmospheric anomalies may be attributed to photographic techniques used during the period, such as long exposure times, which could distort images. Additionally, claims of labor and technology availability are often based on retrospective analyses that may overlook advancements made during the construction processes.
