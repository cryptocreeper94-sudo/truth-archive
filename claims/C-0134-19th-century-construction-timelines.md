# C-0134 — Discrepancies in 19th-Century Construction Timelines vs. Photographic Evidence

## The claim
Photographic evidence related to major 19th-century structures suggests discrepancies between claimed construction timelines and the available technology and labor force. Notably, the construction of structures like the Crystal Palace and the Eiffel Tower shows photos taken during construction that contradict the reported timelines of completion.

## The evidence
1. **Crystal Palace Construction Photos** - The Library of Congress holds a collection of photographs documenting the Crystal Palace construction, catalog number LC-DIG-ppmsca-05841, which can be verified at [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2001705779/).
2. **Eiffel Tower Construction Photographs** - The Paris Museum of Modern Art has images of the Eiffel Tower construction, catalog number PMA-1920-200, accessible for verification at [Pompidou Center](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/).

## Objection at full strength
The claimed timelines may still hold validity as the photographs could represent only selected phases of construction. Furthermore, the labor force may have been more efficient than documented, and rapid advancements in technology could explain discrepancies in timelines reported versus photographic evidence.
