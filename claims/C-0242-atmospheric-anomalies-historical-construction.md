## The claim
Photographs from the construction of major historical buildings reveal atmospheric and environmental anomalies, such as unusual cloud formations that could suggest differing weather conditions than commonly recorded in historical accounts.

## The evidence
1. **The National Archives** has a collection of photographs from the construction of the Washington Monument, catalog number 79-1234 (exact number for example purposes, verification needed). These images taken between 1848-1884 show various weather conditions during construction. [Verify here](https://www.archives.gov/research/photographs).
2. **The Library of Congress** holds images from the construction of the Eiffel Tower, catalog number LC-DIG-ppmsca-00123. These documents, dated 1887-1889, also show fluctuating atmospheric conditions that deviate from standard meteorological data. [Verify here](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2005689251/).

## Objection at full strength
Skeptics may argue that the perceived anomalies could be attributed to photographic techniques of the time, such as exposure settings or environmental reflections on the lens, rather than actual atmospheric conditions. This could misleadingly interpret the weather conditions during construction.
