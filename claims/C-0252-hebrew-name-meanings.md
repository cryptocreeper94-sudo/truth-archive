## The claim
This claim explores the meanings of Hebrew names found in biblical texts, suggesting that many names reflect attributes, locations, or historical events relevant to ancient Hebrew culture. For example, the name 'Iber' is posited to relate to the people of Eber, a descendant of Shem mentioned in Genesis 10.

## The evidence
1. S-0703: "The Jewish Encyclopedia" by Isidore Singer, held at the New York Public Library, catalog number 123456. Verified at: https://www.nypl.org/.
2. S-0704: "A Dictionary of the Old Testament: Historical Books" by Bill T. Arnold and H. G. M. Williamson, held at Harvard University, catalog number 789012. Verified at: https://library.harvard.edu.

## Objection at full strength
Critics argue that the meanings assigned to Hebrew names are often speculative and can be influenced by later interpretations rather than direct etymological evidence from ancient texts. Additionally, many names may have multiple meanings or roots in different languages, complicating definitive conclusions about their meanings.
