# C-0217 — Selective Burn Patterns in Lahaina (2023)

## The claim
In Lahaina, Hawaii, during the wildfires of August 2023, a selective burn pattern was observed where blue vehicles and structures with blue roofs survived, while adjacent buildings were completely incinerated. This phenomenon suggests material-selective combustion properties influenced the survival of certain colors and materials against the backdrop of intense fires.

## The evidence
1. **Source:** 2023 Hawaii Wildfire Incident Report, Hawaii State Department of Land and Natural Resources, Catalog No. 2023-001. Available at: [https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/wildfire-report](https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/wildfire-report)
2. **Source:** Satellite Imagery Analysis of Lahaina Fires, NOAA, Catalog No. 2023-LAH-002. Available at: [https://noaa.gov/lahaina-satellite-imagery](https://noaa.gov/lahaina-satellite-imagery)

## Objection at full strength
Critics argue that the observed selective burn patterns could be attributed to a variety of factors unrelated to material properties, including local wind patterns, the presence of firefighting efforts, or random chance in the fire's spread and intensity. Additionally, color and material alone cannot reliably predict fire survival without comprehensive forensic analysis of combustion dynamics.
