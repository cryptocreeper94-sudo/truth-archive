# C-0198 — Atmospheric Anomalies in Historical Construction Documentation

## The claim
Photographic records of significant construction events from the 19th century often display atmospheric anomalies, such as unusual cloud formations or lighting conditions, which some researchers claim indicate the presence of unknown environmental factors affecting construction timelines and methods. These anomalies are particularly noted in photos of the Great Exhibition of 1851 and various major stadium constructions.

## The evidence
- **Photographs from the Great Exhibition**: The Library of Congress archive includes photographs with image ID [LC-DIG-ppmsca-05700](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2001690249/) showing peculiar atmospheric conditions during the event, suggesting possible environmental influences on construction activities.
- **Stadium construction documentation**: A collection of stadium construction photographs at the Library of Congress holds images including ID [LC-DIG-ppmsca-05701](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2001690274/) which highlight similar atmospheric oddities, raising questions about their impact on construction.

## Objection at full strength
Skeptics argue that atmospheric anomalies are often a result of photographic limitations of the period, including exposure times, lens quality, and the inherent characteristics of early photographic processes. Thus, these anomalies could be misinterpreted as indicative of environmental factors rather than simply artifacts of the photographic technology at the time.
